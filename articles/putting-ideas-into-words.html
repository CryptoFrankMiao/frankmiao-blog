将想法转化为文字

Writing is a strict test that exposes the imprecision and incompleteness of ideas.

将想法转化为文字 --> 2022年2月

写关于某件事的东西，即使是你很了解的事，通常也会让你意识到你并不像自己以为的那样了解它。将想法转化为文字是一个严格的测试。你最初选择的词通常是错的；你必须反复重写句子才能把它们完全写对。而且你的想法不仅会不精确，还会不完整。最终出现在文章中的一半想法，会是你写作时想到的。事实上，这就是我写作的原因。

一旦你发表了什么东西，惯例是认为你写的正是你写作前的想法。这些是你的想法，现在你已经表达了它们。但你知道这不是真的。你知道将想法转化为文字改变了它们。而且不仅仅是那些你发表的想法。想必还有一些想法最终被证明太破碎而无法修复，那些你就丢弃了。

不仅仅是必须将想法落实到具体的词语才让写作如此苛刻。真正的测试是阅读你写的东西。你必须假装成一个中立的读者，对你头脑中的内容一无所知，只知道你写了什么。当他读到你写的东西时，它看起来正确吗？它看起来完整吗？

如果你努力，你可以像一个完全陌生的人一样阅读你的写作，而当你这样做时，消息通常是坏的。我需要很多轮修改才能让一篇文章通过陌生人的检验。但陌生人是理性的，所以如果你问他需要什么，你总是可以满足他。如果他不满意是因为你没有提到 x 或没有充分限定某个句子，那么你就提到 x 或添加更多限定。现在满意了吗？

这可能让你失去一些漂亮的句子，但你必须 resigned 于此。你只需要让它们尽可能好，同时仍然满足陌生人。

这一点，我假设，不会有太大争议。我认为这与任何尝试过写任何非琐碎主题的人的经验相符。可能确实存在那些想法如此完美形成以至于可以直接流入文字的人。但我从未认识过能做到这一点的人，如果我遇到声称能做到的人，这似乎是他们的局限而非能力的证据。

事实上，这是电影中的一个套路：那个声称有一个计划来做某件困难事情的人，当被进一步追问时，敲敲他的头说"都在这儿呢"。每个看电影的人都知道那意味着什么。充其量计划是模糊和不完整的。很可能有一些未发现的缺陷使其完全无效。充其量这是一个计划的计划。

在精确定义的领域中，有可能在头脑中形成完整的想法。人们可以在头脑中下棋，例如。数学家可以在头脑中进行一些数学运算，尽管他们似乎对超过一定长度的证明不太确定，直到他们把它写下来。但这似乎只适用于可以用形式语言表达的想法。

[1] 可以说，这样的人正在他们的头脑中将想法转化为文字。我可以在某种程度上在头脑中写文章。我有时会在走路或躺在床上时想到一段文字，它在最终版本中几乎 unchanged。但实际上我在这样做时就是在写作。我在做写作的心理部分；只是我的手指在这样做时没有移动。

[2] 你可以对某件事了解很多而不写关于它。你能了解得如此之多以至于从试图解释你所知道的中学不到更多吗？我不这么认为。我写过至少两个我很了解的主题——Lisp 黑客和创业——在这两种情况下，我从写作中学到了很多。在这两种情况下，有些东西直到我必须解释它们时我才 consciously 意识到。而且我不认为我的经验是异常的。大量的知识是无意识的，专家的无意识知识比例比初学者更高。

我不是说写作是探索所有想法的最佳方式。如果你有关于建筑的想法， presumably 探索它们的最佳方式是建造 actual 建筑物。我想说的是，无论你从以其他方式探索想法中学到多少，你从写作关于它们中仍然会学到新东西。

将想法转化为文字当然不必意味着写作。你也可以用老式的方式，通过交谈。但根据我的经验，写作是更严格的测试。你必须承诺一个单一的、最佳的词语序列。当你没有语调来承载意义时，更少的东西可以 unsaid。而且你可以以一种在交谈中会显得过度的方式来集中注意力。我经常花两周时间写一篇 essay，重读草稿50次。如果你在交谈中这样做，那似乎是某种精神 disorder 的证据。

如果你懒惰，当然，写作和交谈同样无用。但如果你想推动自己把事情做对，写作是更陡峭的山。

[3] 我花了这么长时间建立这个相当明显的观点的原因是，它导向另一个许多人会觉得震惊的观点。如果写下你的想法总是让它们更精确、更完整，那么没有写过某个主题的人就没有关于它的 fully formed 想法。而从不写作的人对任何非琐碎的事情都没有 fully formed 的想法。

对他们来说感觉好像他们有，特别是如果他们不习惯批判性地审视自己的思考。想法可以感觉完整。只有当你试图将它们转化为文字时，你才会发现它们不是。所以如果你从不将你的想法 subject 于那个测试，你不仅永远不会有 fully formed 的想法，而且永远不会意识到这一点。

将想法转化为文字当然不是保证它们会是正确的。远非如此。但尽管它不是充分条件，它是必要条件。

注释

[1] 机械和电路是形式语言。

[2] 当我在帕洛阿尔托的街上走路时，我想到了这个句子。

[3] 与人交谈有两种含义：严格意义上谈话是口头的，更一般的意义上它可以采取任何形式，包括写作。在极限情况下（例如塞内卡的信件），后一种意义上的交谈变成了 essay 写作。在你写东西时与其他人交谈（无论是哪种意义）可能非常有用。但口头交谈永远不会比当你谈论你正在写的东西时更苛刻。

法文翻译

Putting Ideas into Words --> February 2022 Writing about something, even something you know well, usually shows you that you didn't know it as well as you thought. Putting ideas into words is a severe test. The first words you choose are usually wrong; you have to rewrite sentences over and over to get them exactly right. And your ideas won't just be imprecise, but incomplete too. Half the ideas that end up in an essay will be ones you thought of while you were writing it. Indeed, that's why I write them.

Once you publish something, the convention is that whatever you wrote was what you thought before you wrote it. These were your ideas, and now you've expressed them. But you know this isn't true. You know that putting your ideas into words changed them. And not just the ideas you published. Presumably there were others that turned out to be too broken to fix, and those you discarded instead.

It's not just having to commit your ideas to specific words that makes writing so exacting. The real test is reading what you've written. You have to pretend to be a neutral reader who knows nothing of what's in your head, only what you wrote. When he reads what you wrote, does it seem correct? Does it seem complete? If you make an effort, you can read your writing as if you were a complete stranger, and when you do the news is usually bad. It takes me many cycles before I can get an essay past the stranger. But the stranger is rational, so you always can, if you ask him what he needs. If he's not satisfied because you failed to mention x or didn't qualify some sentence sufficiently, then you mention x or add more qualifications. Happy now? It may cost you some nice sentences, but you have to resign yourself to that. You just have to make them as good as you can and still satisfy the stranger.

This much, I assume, won't be that controversial. I think it will accord with the experience of anyone who has tried to write about anything nontrivial. There may exist people whose thoughts are so perfectly formed that they just flow straight into words. But I've never known anyone who could do this, and if I met someone who said they could, it would seem evidence of their limitations rather than their ability. Indeed, this is a trope in movies: the guy who claims to have a plan for doing some difficult thing, and who when questioned further, taps his head and says "It's all up here." Everyone watching the movie knows what that means. At best the plan is vague and incomplete. Very likely there's some undiscovered flaw that invalidates it completely. At best it's a plan for a plan.

In precisely defined domains it's possible to form complete ideas in your head. People can play chess in their heads, for example. And mathematicians can do some amount of math in their heads, though they don't seem to feel sure of a proof over a certain length till they write it down. But this only seems possible with ideas you can express in a formal language. [ 1 ] Arguably what such people are doing is putting ideas into words in their heads. I can to some extent write essays in my head. I'll sometimes think of a paragraph while walking or lying in bed that survives nearly unchanged in the final version. But really I'm writing when I do this. I'm doing the mental part of writing; my fingers just aren't moving as I do it. [ 2 ]

You can know a great deal about something without writing about it. Can you ever know so much that you wouldn't learn more from trying to explain what you know? I don't think so. I've written about at least two subjects I know well — Lisp hacking and startups — and in both cases I learned a lot from writing about them. In both cases there were things I didn't consciously realize till I had to explain them. And I don't think my experience was anomalous. A great deal of knowledge is unconscious, and experts have if anything a higher proportion of unconscious knowledge than beginners. I'm not saying that writing is the best way to explore all ideas. If you have ideas about architecture, presumably the best way to explore them is to build actual buildings. What I'm saying is that however much you learn from exploring ideas in other ways, you'll still learn new things from writing about them.

Putting ideas into words doesn't have to mean writing, of course. You can also do it the old way, by talking. But in my experience, writing is the stricter test. You have to commit to a single, optimal sequence of words. Less can go unsaid when you don't have tone of voice to carry meaning. And you can focus in a way that would seem excessive in conversation. I'll often spend 2 weeks on an essay and reread drafts 50 times. If you did that in conversation it would seem evidence of some kind of mental disorder. If you're lazy, of course, writing and talking are equally useless. But if you want to push yourself to get things right, writing is the steeper hill. [ 3 ]

The reason I've spent so long establishing this rather obvious point is that it leads to another that many people will find shocking. If writing down your ideas always makes them more precise and more complete, then no one who hasn't written about a topic has fully formed ideas about it. And someone who never writes has no fully formed ideas about anything nontrivial. It feels to them as if they do, especially if they're not in the habit of critically examining their own thinking. Ideas can feel complete. It's only when you try to put them into words that you discover they're not. So if you never subject your ideas to that test, you'll not only never have fully formed ideas, but also never realize it. Putting ideas into words is certainly no guarantee that they'll be right. Far from it. But though it's not a sufficient condition, it is a necessary one.

Notes [ 1 ] Machinery and circuits are formal languages. [ 2 ] I thought of this sentence as I was walking down the street in Palo Alto. [ 3 ] There are two senses of talking to someone: a strict sense in which the conversation is verbal, and a more general sense in which it can take any form, including writing. In the limit case (e.g. Seneca's letters), conversation in the latter sense becomes essay writing. It can be very useful to talk (in either sense) with other people as you're writing something. But a verbal conversation will never be more exacting than when you're talking about something you're writing. 核心洞察：

写作是严格的测试，暴露想法的不精确和不完整。没有写过某个话题的人就没有完全形成关于它的想法。

最有力的观点：

"If writing down your ideas always makes them more precise and more complete, then no one who hasn't written about a topic has fully formed ideas about it."

（如果写下你的想法总是让它们更精确、更完整，那么没有写过某个主题的人就没有关于它的完全形成的想法。）

个人反思：

这也解释了为什么写作对于投资分析如此重要。只有通过写作，你才能真正理清自己的投资逻辑。
